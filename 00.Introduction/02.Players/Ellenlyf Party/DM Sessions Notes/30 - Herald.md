# Session 30 — _Herald_

**Format:** Return of the Lazy DM **Structure:** Three acts — T=0, The Aftermath, The Lake **Tone:** This is the ending. Make it feel like one. **Theme Question:** _What do you do with what you couldn't stop?_

**Hard Rule:** Don't rush any of the three acts. Each one needs to breathe. The arc has been ten sessions. Give the landing the weight it earned.

**Hidden DM Rule:** Nobody wins. Nobody loses. Something ends and something begins. The party carries both out of Barovia.

---

## Before The Session — The Rafi Conversation

_Pull Rafi aside before anyone sits down._

Session 30 is the last session in Barovia. Kairos's secret — what Damian told him on the Pequod — has one more chance to surface before the arc closes.

Tell Rafi:

> _"Cyric is at the lake tonight. He's the God of Lies. He almost certainly knows what you know. Whether he says it out loud in front of the party is up to me — but your reaction to him will tell the party something regardless. I'm not telling you what to do. I just want you to know the moment is coming."_

Then let him decide what Kairos does.

## Strong Start

_Open on the moon._

Not the party. Not HAWA. The moon.

It's moving. The players can see it even from the sewer — the quality of light filtering through the drainage grates is wrong. Too direct. Too overhead. The Festival crowd above them can hear it — not a sound exactly, but a collective intake of breath as thousands of people look up simultaneously.

[[HAWA]] is already standing.

She has been awake for thirty hours. She has the Stone in her hand. She looks at the party — not for permission, not for consensus, just looks at them the way someone looks at people they have tried to protect before doing something they cannot be protected from.

She says:

> _"Selûne. It is time."_

She raises the Stone.

**The moon breaks.**

## Act 1 — T=0

### What Happens

Not a beam descending. Not targeted moonlight. The moon itself repositions — a goddess's body, anchored against her will for a century, commanded by the Stone to move to direct overhead. The accumulated strain of a hundred years releases at once.

The moon _shatters._

Pieces of it drift slowly in the sky above Barovia — wrong, fractured, the light they cast fragmented and irregular. It will not be whole again. Not in this arc. Maybe not ever.

**From the shattered moon, Selûne arrives.**

Not through a crack in the anchor. Through [[HAWA]].

[[HAWA]] floats. The Stone drops from her hand and hits the sewer floor. And the goddess who has been imprisoned in this plane for a century pours herself into the vessel of the woman who — while trying to free her — imprisoned her further.

**[[HAWA]]'s voice changes.** Her face changes. She is still there — somewhere inside — but Selûne is _present_ in a way that makes everything else feel thin.

### Selûne's Fury — Released, Not Directed

She is not choosing targets. She is _out_ after a hundred years and the release of that is not controlled.

The light above Barovia blazes — turned to eleven, the suppression Strahd built into the system undone completely. Every vampire in the open: gone. Vaporized not as punishment but as the natural consequence of a goddess's full presence burning away what her light was designed to burn.

Mortals in the open: scorched. The Festival crowd scatters toward doorways, alleys, shadows. Some make it. Some don't. The ones who trusted the compact enough to stand next to vampires in the street — they're the ones caught furthest from cover.

**The party:**

They're in the sewer or just emerging from it. Walls around them. Partial cover. They feel the heat — genuinely, physically, spell slot levels of radiant damage appropriate to their situation — but they survive because geometry protected them, not because Selûne chose to spare them.

**[[Sephire]]:**

He's been down outside the Silent Inn since T=30 minutes. He's conscious when the moon breaks — barely — and he sees it happen from ground level in the market. He finds cover. He survives. He is the first person in the party to see the full scope of what just happened.

### Selûne Speaks

The fury exhausts itself. Selûne's presence in [[HAWA]]'s body is already beginning to withdraw — a goddess cannot stay anchored in a mortal vessel for long, and the anchor that held her here is broken.

She looks at the party. All of them. Through [[HAWA]]'s eyes.

She says — not in anger, in the specific grief of someone who has been failed by people who meant well:

> _"A hundred years. And you could not stop one woman who believed."_

A beat. Then quieter, already beginning to leave:

> _"Herald of Time. The other gods have named you. Remember what you could not stop here — when you face what comes next."_

She releases [[HAWA]].

[[HAWA]] drops. The Stone hits the ground.

The moon above Barovia is broken. The registration system is gone. The coexistence compact is gone. Barovia is not what it was when the party arrived.

### What The Black Hood Society Does

Nothing.

That's what makes it terrifying.

The most powerful administrative apparatus in Barovia — the organization that has run this plane for eighty years through proxies and ledgers and warrants — stands in a goddess's fury with absolutely no protocol for this.

You cannot file a warrant against Selûne.

The ones who were in the open at the Festival: gone or burning. The ones who were inside the Castle or in buildings: alive, intact, staring at each other across a broken organizational structure with no leadership and no records and no system left to administrate.

They scatter. Not in coordination. Individually. For the first time in eighty years, the Black Hood Society makes decisions based on personal survival rather than institutional mandate.

Radovan, wherever he is, opens his notebook. He writes something. He closes it.

He doesn't run. He recalibrates.

## Act 2 — The Aftermath

### The Stone on the Ground

This is the scene. Everything else has been building to this moment.

The Stone is on the sewer floor. [[HAWA]] is down. The party is together — or getting there, finding each other through the chaos, converging on this location.

**Give them a moment of quiet.** The session has been loud. Let the quiet land.

Then: **the choice.**

What do they do with the Stone?

Do not rush this. Do not suggest options. Let the party talk.

If they need prompting — Sephire walks in. He's been down and up and he's seen the full scope of what just happened from ground level. He looks at the Stone. He looks at [[HAWA]] on the floor.

He says what he came to say — what he's been trying to get to for three sessions:

> _"The mummified creature in Ravenloft told me. The Stone is Strahd. He turned himself into it."_

**And then — if Kairos hasn't spoken yet — this is the moment.**

Because if the Stone is Strahd, and the stones feed Ao's plan to absorb divine power, then delivering the Stone to the Bureau means delivering Strahd to Ao. And Kairos has known what Ao does with the stones since Shattar-Kai.

The room either explodes or goes very quiet. Either is correct.

**The four options and their consequences:**

**Keep it** — They carry Strahd through the Astral Sea. Cyric will find this very amusing. The Bureau will want to know where it is. ADAM, when he finds out, will have a response.

**Deliver to Bureau** — Mission complete. Ao gets what he needs. The Second Time of Trouble moves one step closer. Kairos has been carrying the knowledge of what this means since Shattar-Kai.

**Give to ADAM** — Strahd's son holds his father. ADAM doesn't ask for it. But if offered he takes it without explanation. He has maintained his father's domain for a hundred years. This is one more weight.

**Destroy it** — Can they? It's a Netherese artifact. What happens to Strahd if it's destroyed? What happens to what remains of Selûne's anchor?

Whatever they decide — **they decide it here.** Not at the lake. Not in the Astral Sea. In the sewer, with [[HAWA]] on the floor and the broken moon above them and thirty sessions of consequences sitting in the room.

### HAWA's Final Scene

She's alive. Barely. Selûne used her and left and what remains is a woman who has been awake for thirty hours and was briefly a goddess's anchor and woke up in the rubble of what her certainty caused.

She doesn't immediately speak. She looks at the Stone. At the party. At whatever remains of the light coming through the drainage grates above.

When she does speak it's quiet. Not the calm of certainty. The calm of someone whose certainty has left them and they're discovering what's underneath it.

She says something true. Let it be the last true thing she says in Barovia:

> _"I thought if I was righteous enough, the consequences would be righteous too."_

She doesn't ask forgiveness. She doesn't justify. She sits with what she's done.

What the party does with her is their choice. She won't stop them from anything. She is, for the first time in ten sessions, genuinely uncertain about what happens next.

### ADAM — Outside The Walls

He felt it. He saw the moon break from outside Vallaki's gate. He has been standing there since Sephire went in.

**He doesn't come to find them.** He waits at the lake. He knows they'll come — there's only one way out of Barovia and it's the Pequod.

When the party arrives at the lake and sees him standing by the water — before they see Cyric — ADAM looks at whoever is carrying the Stone. Or not carrying it.

He doesn't ask what happened to it. He can see the broken moon. He knows.

He says one thing:

> _"Was it worth it?"_

Not accusingly. He genuinely wants to know. He has been maintaining his father's domain for a hundred years for reasons he has never fully explained to anyone. He needs to know if the alternative was worth what it cost.

The party's answer — whatever it is — is the last thing ADAM hears before Cyric appears.

## Act 3 — The Lake

### The Road Through Vallaki

Getting from the sewer to Lake Zarovich takes the party through a changed city.

Not violent. Not on fire. _Stunned._

The Black Hood Society's administrative grip is gone overnight. The Crown Prince has no handlers. The registered vampires who survived are unregistered — their marks gone, their structure gone, their compact gone, their hunger managed by nothing. The mortals who survived are standing in streets that look the same as they did this morning and are completely different.

Nobody knows what Barovia is anymore.

The party walks through it. Let them see specific things:

- A registered vampire sitting against a wall in the aftermath, looking at his wrist where the mark used to be. Not attacking anyone. Just sitting.
- A mortal family standing in a doorway looking at the fractured moon. Not speaking.
- A section of the Festival street that is simply empty now — the booths still decorated, the food still on the tables, the chairs tipped over, nobody there.
- The Silent Inn. Still standing. The sign language rule still posted on the door. Nobody inside.

No encounters. No combat. Just the walk.

Sephire knows this road. He walked it at T=30 minutes heading in. He walks it now heading out. It is not the same road.

### Cyric at the Lake

He's sitting on a rock by the water. Not dramatically positioned. Not surrounded by darkness or divine light. Just sitting, like someone who arrived early and has been comfortable waiting.

The Pequod is visible at the dock. ADAM is nearby.

[[faerunian-cyric-scag|Cyric]] looks at the party as they approach — takes his time, looks at each of them — and says:

> _"Herald of Time. I've been wanting to meet you."_

He is genuinely delighted. Not threatening. Delighted the way Littlefinger is delighted when the board becomes interesting.

**He tells them three things:**

**First — What Herald of Time means:**

The gods have been watching since Artifact 1. They didn't have a name for the party until Shattar-Kai. _Herald of Time_ means they are present at every significant moment leading to a new age — not because they're powerful enough to cause it, but because they are the _witnesses who become participants who become catalysts._ Every plane they've touched has changed. Not always in the way they intended. But changed.

He says this like it's a compliment. It might be.

**Second — The Second Time of Trouble:**

Ao's plan with the six stones is not just power collection. It is the mechanism for the Second Time of Trouble — the gods stripped of divine power, forced to walk as mortals, the divine order disrupted and remade.

He smiles when he says this. It is not a sympathetic smile.

> _"Last time was rather entertaining. I have high hopes for the sequel."_

He is the God of Strife and Lies and Chaos. The Second Time of Trouble is not a threat to him. It is an opportunity. He wants it to happen. He thinks the party might cause it.

He thinks this is wonderful.

**Third — The final stone:**

It's in the Astral Sea. He won't say where exactly. But he suggests — pleasantly, as if offering friendly advice — that when they meet whoever sent them to collect stones, they ask better questions than they've been asking.

Then he looks at whoever is holding the Stone — or at the empty space where it would be if they destroyed it or gave it away — and says:

> _"Interesting choice."_

Whether that's a compliment or a condemnation is genuinely unclear. He doesn't clarify.

**The Kairos moment:**

Cyric looks at Kairos specifically, for just a beat longer than he looks at anyone else.

He doesn't say what Damian told Kairos on the Pequod. He doesn't expose the secret.

He just looks. And then looks away.

The party may or may not register this. Kairos will.

**Cyric leaves:**

Not dramatically. He isn't there anymore. No flash of light, no divine departure. Just — gone. The way a person leaves a conversation when they've said what they came to say.

The lake is quiet.

ADAM is still there. He watched the whole conversation without speaking.

### Boarding the Pequod

This is the arc's final image.

The party boards. The Pequod prepares to leave. Barovia recedes behind them — the fractured moon above it wrong and drifting, the city visible in the distance, the lake going still as the ship moves onto the Astral currents.

**Give each character one beat as Barovia disappears:**

Not monologues. Not speeches. One image or one line per character that captures what they're carrying out of this plane.

**[[Sephire]]** — He was the first one hit by the moonwave. He saw the full scope from ground level. He looks at the broken moon until he can't see it anymore.

**[[Minerva]]** — She covered Alice with her robes on the stairs. She walked through a door and didn't go back. She looks at her hands.

**[[Froggo]]** — He received Selûne's dream. He negotiated with Basilisks and shot them. He has lived for centuries looking for meaning and a goddess whispered to him in his sleep. He looks at the moon — broken now — and something in him recognizes the voice from the dream.

**[[Kairos]]** — Cyric looked at him. Just for a beat. He knows what that look meant. He stands at the rail and says nothing. Again.

**[[Asep]]** — He killed someone with a Magic Missile. He got 100 gold for it. He has been wearing a uniform that wasn't his for three sessions. He takes it off somewhere on deck and leaves it.

**[[Verdian Suyanti|Venty]]** — He moved a string for aesthetic reasons and accidentally solved a mystery. He watched two vampires vaporize in a basement while he was sitting with them. He tidies something on the deck that doesn't need tidying.

The Pequod leaves Barovia.

Somewhere ahead in the Astral Sea, Awanama is waiting in a ship.

## Secrets & Clues

Reveal all of these. This is the finale. Everything lands tonight.

1. **The Stone is Strahd** — Sephire delivers this. If he doesn't for some reason, Selûne's presence in [[HAWA]] makes it clear — she recognizes her anchor.
2. **Herald of Time** — Selûne names them. Cyric confirms it.
3. **Ao's plan** — Cyric describes the Second Time of Trouble mechanism. Whether Kairos confirms it with what Damian told him is Rafi's choice.
4. **HAWA's truth** — _"I thought if I was righteous enough, the consequences would be righteous too."_ This is her. Finally.
5. **ADAM's question** — _"Was it worth it?"_ He needs to know. Whatever they answer, he accepts it.
6. **The final stone is in the Astral Sea** — Cyric confirms this. Act 2 begins.

## Important NPCs

**[[HAWA]]** She is no longer certain. This is new. She has been certain for her entire adult life. The absence of certainty is not peace — it's unfamiliar and frightening and she is sitting in the rubble of what her certainty caused trying to figure out what she is now. She doesn't need forgiveness from the party. She doesn't ask for it. She needs someone to sit with her in the uncertainty for a moment before everything moves on. _Does the party give her that? Entirely up to them._

**[[ADAM|Warga Lokal]]** He watched the moon break from outside the gate. He has been maintaining his father's domain for a hundred years. He is at the lake not because he followed the party but because the lake is the only place to go when the city behind you has changed into something you don't recognize. His question — _"Was it worth it?"_ — is the most honest thing he has said to anyone since ADAM told Sephire about HAWA in the tunnel. _He is not looking for comfort. He is looking for an honest answer._

**[[Radovan Czeslav Voss]]** Somewhere in Vallaki. Still writing. His report just became the most significant document in Barovia's history and nobody will read it because the organization it was written for has scattered. He will find something to do with himself. He always does. _He is not in Session 30 unless the party seeks him out. But he exists._

**Cyric** God of Strife, Lies, and Chaos. Genuinely delighted by the situation. Does not want to stop the Second Time of Trouble — wants to _cause_ it, or at minimum wants the party to cause it. His conversation at the lake is the most honest he has been with anyone in centuries, which means approximately sixty percent of it is true and the rest is carefully calibrated. The Littlefinger read: he invested in the party because they're interesting. He wants to see what they do next. _He is not their enemy. He is not their ally. He is watching._

The walk through Vallaki is specifically encounter-free. The lake is specifically encounter-free. Let the weight do the work.

## How To End The Session

End when the Pequod clears Barovia's planar boundary and the fractured moon is no longer visible.

**Final line:**

_Read this aloud. Slowly._

> _The last thing visible through the Pequod's stern is the moon above Barovia — broken, wrong, pieces of it drifting slowly in the dark above a plane that does not know yet what it has become._
> 
> _Somewhere in that city: [[HAWA]], sitting in the rubble of her certainty._ _Somewhere in the sewer: [[Alexander|Adrianna]], or what the door cost her._ _Somewhere at the gate: [[ADAM|Warga Lokal]], alone with a domain that is no longer what his father built._
> 
> _You carry Barovia with you._ _You always will._
> 
> _Ahead: the Astral Sea. The final stone. Awanama, waiting._ _The gods are moving._ _So are you._
> 
> _End of the Barovia Arc._

## Last Note — For You, Not Them

Ten sessions. You said it was the weakest arc. You were wrong about weak. You were right that it stretched.

What you built in ten sessions:

A woman who caused a catastrophe through faith. A man who maintained a broken system for love. A goddess imprisoned in her own moonlight. A god who finds the end of everything amusing. A party who arrived to help and left as the Herald of Time.

The Spec Ops mirror held. Walker didn't know he was the catastrophe. Your players know. That's the difference between a story that implicates and a story that merely entertains.

They're going to carry Barovia into Act 2. Every choice they made here follows them through the Astral Sea to the final stone.

That's not a weak arc. That's a foundation.

**Run Session 30 like it's the last session of a story that mattered. Because it is.**
